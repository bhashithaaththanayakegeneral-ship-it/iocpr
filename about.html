About IOCPR

Building a world where reform replaces punishment.

The International Organization for Crime Prevention and Rehabilitation is a registered NGO founded in 2025, headquartered in Battaramulla, Sri Lanka — working locally, regionally and internationally.

Our story

A response to an escalating global crisis.

Crime fractures families, drains communities and traps individuals in cycles that are hard to escape. IOCPR was founded on a simple conviction: that prevention, rehabilitation and opportunity do more to build safe societies than punishment alone.

We work with marginalized communities, support victims, and help reformed individuals reintegrate into society — building collaborative networks that connect governments, NGOs and citizens around a shared goal.

Message from the Chairman

The realization of a peaceful and sustainable world depends upon our collective ability to build societies that are free from crime, violence, and injustice. Crimes against humanity, violence against women and children, cybercrime, drug trafficking, genocide, and other transnational forms of criminality continue to threaten peace, security, human dignity, and sustainable development across the globe.

At a time when humanity is confronted with complex social challenges and the gradual erosion of moral and ethical values, the active engagement of civil society has become more essential than ever. Strengthening justice, promoting social cohesion, protecting human rights, and advancing a culture of peace require meaningful partnerships that transcend national boundaries.

It is with this vision and commitment that the International Organization for Crime Prevention and Rehabilitation (IOCPR) was established. As an independent international organization, we are dedicated to supporting crime prevention, promoting rehabilitation, and fostering safer, more resilient communities in Sri Lanka and around the world.

Working in close collaboration with governments, law enforcement agencies, academic institutions, international organizations, civil society, and community partners, we strive to develop sustainable initiatives that address the root causes of crime while encouraging rehabilitation, reintegration, and restorative justice. We firmly believe that preventing crime is a shared responsibility and that lasting peace can only be achieved through collective action.

Our commitment extends beyond the present generation. We work with the vision of safeguarding the future of our children and generations yet unborn by contributing to the creation of a just, peaceful, inclusive, and compassionate global society.

I respectfully invite individuals, institutions, and partners across the world to join us in this important mission. Together, let us build a safer world where justice prevails, human dignity is protected, and every individual has the opportunity to live in peace.

Message from the Secretary General

Crime is not merely the consequence of an individual's personal choices. It is often the result of a complex interplay of social, economic, political, cultural, and, at times, biological factors. While the law rightly provides for punishment where offences are committed, lasting public safety can only be achieved by addressing the root causes of crime and by creating meaningful opportunities for rehabilitation and reintegration.

Many offenders emerge from environments marked by social inequality, poverty, unequal access to opportunities, inadequate socialization, fractured family structures, and limited personal development. Recognizing these realities reminds us that crime prevention is not solely the responsibility of governments, law enforcement agencies, or judicial institutions. It is a shared responsibility that calls for the active participation of every sector of society.

Founded in 2025, the International Organization for Crime Prevention and Rehabilitation (IOCPR) was established with the vision of promoting community participation in crime prevention across Sri Lanka, throughout the region, and around the world. Our mission is to unite professionals, academics, practitioners, volunteers, and communities in building safer, more inclusive, and more compassionate societies.

To achieve this vision, the Organization is committed to promoting community-based crime prevention initiatives, supporting innovative rehabilitation and offender reintegration programmes, conducting research and policy dialogue, organizing educational programmes, seminars, workshops, and conferences, providing legal guidance and assistance where appropriate, strengthening support for victims of crime and witnesses, and advancing the protection of human rights and the rule of law.

As we move forward, I warmly invite individuals, institutions, and communities to join us in this important mission. Through collaboration, shared responsibility, and unwavering commitment, we can create a society where justice, peace, equality, and human dignity prevail.

Together, let us build a safer world—one free from crime, founded on justice, compassion, and respect for every human being.

Senior Lecturer Ravindra Samarawickrama Secretary General International Organization for Crime Prevention and Rehabilitation (IOCPR)

Senior Advisor's Message

The growing complexity of crime in the modern world has made the need for a comprehensive, systematic, and collaborative approach to crime prevention, reduction, and control more critical than ever before. This profound responsibility cannot rest solely with governments or law enforcement agencies; rather, it demands the collective commitment of policymakers, academics, legal professionals, researchers, civil society, and every responsible citizen. A just and effective criminal justice system must be founded on the principles of fairness, accountability, inclusiveness, and respect for the rule of law.

In Sri Lanka, there is an urgent need to strengthen criminological research, legal scholarship, and interdisciplinary academic dialogue within our universities and higher education institutions. Encouraging evidence-based research, scholarly discussions, and professional collaboration will contribute significantly to understanding emerging crime trends and developing sustainable strategies for crime prevention and criminal justice reform.

Across the world, innovative research and the exchange of knowledge remain essential to strengthening rehabilitation and social reintegration as fundamental pillars of effective crime prevention. By promoting rehabilitation alongside justice, we not only reduce reoffending but also create safer communities and a more compassionate society.

I firmly believe that the International Organization for Crime Prevention and Rehabilitation (IOCPR) has a significant responsibility in advancing this global mission. Through research, education, professional development, international cooperation, and community engagement, the Organization can make a meaningful contribution towards building a safer, more just, and peaceful world for present and future generations.

Senior Lecturer Udaya Kumara Amarasinghe Department of Criminology and Criminal Justice Senior Advisor International Organization for Crime Prevention and Rehabilitation (IOCPR)

Our Mission

To prevent crime and rehabilitate offenders through education, advocacy and collaboration — restoring dignity to individuals and safety to communities.

Our Vision

A world without crime, where every person — including those who have erred — has the opportunity to reform, contribute and live with dignity.

Our pillars

Four commitments at the heart of our work

Prevention

Reduce crime through awareness and education.

Reformation

Rehabilitate offenders via education and vocational programmes.

Advocacy

Champion human rights and social justice.

Collaboration

Foster partnerships between governments, NGOs and communities.
